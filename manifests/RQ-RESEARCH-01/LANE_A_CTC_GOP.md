## 1. Executive Summary & Core Research Questions

### 1.1 Context and Problem Statement
In Computer-Assisted Pronunciation Training (CAPT) and Mispronunciation Detection & Diagnosis (MDD), second-language (L2) learners frequently exhibit phonemic errors spanning substitutions, distortions, epenthetic insertions, and phoneme omissions (deletions). In our target population—particularly Vietnamese learners of English—phoneme deletions (especially word-final coda consonant deletions such as `/T/`, `/D/`, `/S/`, `/Z/`, `/L/`) account for over **53.57%** of all omitted phonemes and **14.93%** of all pronunciation errors in the L2-ARCTIC corpus.

However, existing automated assessment models suffer from a fundamental architectural limitation when dealing with omissions:
1. **Classical Goodness of Pronunciation (GOP: Witt & Young, 2000)** and **Neural Transformer GOP (GOPT: Gong et al., 2022)** rely strictly on pre-segmented acoustic boundaries $[t_s, t_e]$ generated by external Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) forced aligners (e.g., Kaldi TDNN-F). When a phoneme is omitted by a learner, there is **zero acoustic manifestation** ($\Delta t = 0$). Forced aligners either fail catastrophically, compress the missing phone into arbitrary 10–20 ms boundary noise, or stretch neighboring phones over silence, producing uncalibrated, noisy scalar scores.
2. **Langfens Baseline R** (Wav2Vec2 backbone + Cross-Attention + Binary MLP) exhibits a catastrophic false-negative collapse on omissions: validating at an omission Average Precision (AP) of **0.0305** and a missed-omission rate of **97.5%** at threshold $\tau = 0.50$.
3. **Experiment E1 (Skip-Token Cross-Attention)** attempted to resolve this by prepending a learnable acoustic skip token $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$ to cross-attention key/values. As demonstrated in `manifests/RQ-MODEL-01/PROCESS_CHECKPOINT.md`, the skip mass on deletions completely collapsed to **0.000000** ($0.0\%$), and omission AP degraded further by **-7.90%** relative.
4. **The Naive CTC Blank Hypothesis** (claiming that frame-level CTC blank posterior $P(\text{blank} \mid \mathbf{h}_t) > 0.84$ provides high-confidence omission detection) was empirically refuted in **RQ1-DIAG-02B** (`manifests/RQ1-DIAG-02B/SCIENTIFIC_REPORT.md`): on 66 duration-matched omission vs. control pairs, the blank ROC-AUC was only **0.653**, because blank posteriors dominate normal unvoiced speech and inter-phoneme transitions on correct phones as well (mean $P(\text{blank}) = 0.7322$ on correct controls vs. $0.8244$ on omissions).

### 1.2 Core Research Question
*How can an end-to-end, reference-based MDD system accurately detect and diagnose phoneme omissions and substitutions without relying on gold TextGrid timestamps or fragile external forced alignment, while maintaining real-time inference latency (<300 ms) for the Langfens Speaking web service and staying within the 30-hour Tesla T4 GPU compute budget?*

## 2. Foundational Literature Review & Mathematical Formulations

### 2.1 Classical Goodness of Pronunciation (Witt & Young, 2000; P02)
Classical Goodness of Pronunciation (GOP), formulated by Silke M. Witt and Steve J. Young in *Speech Communication* (2000), evaluates the pronunciation quality of an individual canonical phoneme $p_i$ given an acoustic segment $\mathbf{O} = (\mathbf{o}_{t_s}, \dots, \mathbf{o}_{t_e})$ extracted via forced alignment:

$$\text{GOP}(p_i) = \frac{1}{|t_e - t_s + 1|} \log P(p_i \mid \mathbf{O})$$

Using Bayes’ Rule:

$$P(p_i \mid \mathbf{O}) = \frac{P(\mathbf{O} \mid p_i) P(p_i)}{P(\mathbf{O})} = \frac{P(\mathbf{O} \mid p_i) P(p_i)}{\sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} P(\mathbf{O} \mid q) P(q)}$$

Under the standard assumption of equal phoneme priors ($P(p_i) \approx P(q)$) and approximating the denominator via Viterbi decoding over an unconstrained phoneme loop $\mathcal{Q}$:

$$\text{GOP}(p_i) \approx \frac{1}{|t_e - t_s + 1|} \left[ \log P(\mathbf{O} \mid p_i) - \max_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \log P(\mathbf{O} \mid q) \right]$$

#### Theoretical Limitations:
- **Mandatory Pre-Segmentation:** The formula strictly conditions on start and end frames $[t_s, t_e]$. It cannot evaluate phonemes without prior acoustic segmentation.
- **Deletions Violate Bayes Denominator:** When a sound is omitted, $P(\mathbf{O} \mid p_i)$ evaluates background room silence or adjacent formant transitions. The unconstrained phone loop $\max_q \log P(\mathbf{O} \mid q)$ picks whichever silence or background model best fits the noise, rendering the likelihood ratio uncalibrated.
- **Scalar Diagnostic Incapacity:** Classical GOP outputs a single scalar score per segment. It cannot identify the nature of the error (whether the phone was substituted with another phoneme, distorted, or deleted).

### 2.2 Neural & Transformer Goodness of Pronunciation (GOPT: Gong et al., 2022; P06)
Gong et al. (MIT CSAIL / ETS, ICASSP 2022) extended classical GOP into a deep neural framework: the Goodness of Pronunciation Transformer (GOPT).

#### Architectural Pipeline:
1. **Acoustic Front-End:** Uses a Kaldi TDNN-F acoustic model trained on 960 hours of LibriSpeech with 42 pure phoneme targets.
2. **Feature Extraction:** Computes an 84-dimensional GOP feature vector $\mathbf{f}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{84}$ for each phoneme $p_i$ over its forced-alignment interval $[t_s, t_e]$:
   - 42 Log Phone Posteriors (LPP):
     $$\text{LPP}(p) = \frac{1}{t_e - t_s + 1} \sum_{t=t_s}^{t_e} \log P(p \mid \mathbf{o}_t)$$
   - 42 Log Posterior Ratios (LPR) relative to target $p_i$:
     $$\text{LPR}(p_j \mid p_i) = \text{LPP}(p_j) - \text{LPP}(p_i) = \log \frac{P(p_j \mid \mathbf{O})}{P(p_i \mid \mathbf{O})}$$
   - Feature vector: $\mathbf{f}_i = [\text{LPP}(p_1), \dots, \text{LPP}(p_{42}), \text{LPR}(p_1 \mid p_i), \dots, \text{LPR}(p_{42} \mid p_i)]$.
3. **Transformer Scoring Model:**
   - Projects $\mathbf{f}_i$ to dimension $d=24$.
   - Adds a 24-dimensional canonical phoneme embedding $\mathbf{e}(p_i)$ and a trainable positional embedding $\mathbf{p}_i$.
   - Prepends 5 trainable `[CLS]` tokens representing utterance-level aspects: accuracy, completeness, fluency, prosody, and total score.
   - Passes the sequence through a 3-layer Transformer encoder ($d_{\text{model}}=24, N_{\text{heads}}=2$).
   - Hierarchical multi-task regression heads predict phoneme accuracy (0–2 scale), word scores, and utterance scores using MSE loss.

#### Crucial Theoretical & Empirical Limitations:
- **Speechocean762 Has No Deletion Tags:** The Speechocean762 corpus used by GOPT contains continuous perceptual ratings (0–2 for phonemes, 0–10 for words/utterances). It does **not** annotate phoneme deletions or omissions.
- **Completeness Evaluation Collapsed:** In Table 1 of Gong et al. (2022), GOPT achieved Pearson correlation coefficients (PCC) of $0.612$ on phoneme accuracy and $0.742$ on utterance total score, but its utterance **Completeness PCC collapsed to 0.155** (LibriSpeech model) and **0.011** (PAII-A model). The authors hypothesized this was due to class imbalance, but the root cause is structural: Kaldi forced alignment forces every canonical phone onto an interval, making the acoustic features insensitive to omitted speech.
- **Heavyweight External Runtime:** Running GOPT requires Kaldi binaries (C++ compile, OpenFST, ATLAS/OpenBLAS), TDNN-F models, and multi-step shell scripts. This is unfeasible for a lightweight, modular web service.

### 2.3 CTC Posterior Scoring & End-to-End MDD (Peng et al., 2021; P03; Graves et al., 2006)
Peng et al. (Interspeech 2021) investigated fine-tuning self-supervised Wav2Vec 2.0 architectures for MDD on TIMIT and L2-ARCTIC using Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC).

#### Mathematical Formulation of CTC:
Given an acoustic representation $\mathbf{H} = (\mathbf{h}_1, \dots, \mathbf{h}_T)$ from the Wav2Vec2 encoder and a target vocabulary $\mathcal{V}' = \mathcal{V} \cup \{\epsilon\}$ where $\epsilon$ denotes the blank token:
The conditional probability of an alignment path $\boldsymbol{\pi} = (\pi_1, \dots, \pi_T) \in \mathcal{V}'^T$ is:

$$P(\boldsymbol{\pi} \mid \mathbf{X}) = \prod_{t=1}^T P(\pi_t \mid \mathbf{h}_t) = \prod_{t=1}^T \frac{\exp(\mathbf{w}_{\pi_t}^\top \mathbf{h}_t)}{\sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}'} \exp(\mathbf{w}_v^\top \mathbf{h}_t)}$$

CTC defines a many-to-one collapse operator $\mathcal{B}: \mathcal{V}'^T \to \mathcal{V}^{\le T}$ that merges consecutive identical tokens and removes all blanks (e.g., $\mathcal{B}(\epsilon, \text{k}, \text{k}, \epsilon, \text{ae}, \text{t}, \text{t}, \epsilon) = (\text{k}, \text{ae}, \text{t})$).
The sequence objective maximizes the marginal probability of the target label sequence $\mathbf{Y}$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CTC}} = -\log P(\mathbf{Y} \mid \mathbf{X}) = -\log \sum_{\boldsymbol{\pi} \in \mathcal{B}^{-1}(\mathbf{Y})} P(\boldsymbol{\pi} \mid \mathbf{X})$$

#### How Peng et al. Perform MDD:
Crucially, Peng et al. **did not use frame-level blank posteriors or forced-alignment intervals**.
Instead, they formulated MDD as **Free-Phone Recognition followed by Sequence Dynamic Programming**:
1. Greedy CTC decoding generates the recognized phoneme hypothesis:
   $$\hat{\mathbf{Y}} = \mathcal{B}\left(\arg\max_{v \in \mathcal{V}'} P(v \mid \mathbf{h}_t)\right)$$
2. A Levenshtein dynamic programming string alignment aligns the recognized hypothesis $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}$ with the canonical reference prompt $\mathbf{Y}^*$:
   - If canonical $Y^*_i$ matches hypothesis $\hat{Y}_j$: **Correct (True Accept)**.
   - If canonical $Y^*_i$ aligns to hypothesis $\hat{Y}_j \neq Y^*_i$: **Substitution (Mispronunciation detected, diagnosed as $\hat{Y}_j$)**.
   - If canonical $Y^*_i$ aligns to null symbol $\epsilon$: **Deletion / Omission (Mispronunciation detected as omitted)**.
   - If hypothesis $\hat{Y}_j$ aligns to null symbol $\epsilon$ in canonical: **Epenthetic Insertion**.

Using this segmentation-free formulation, Peng et al. achieved an F1 score of **60.44%** on L2-ARCTIC (using Wav2Vec2-XLSR + TIMIT), vastly outperforming classical GOP ($42.42\%$).

### 2.4 Articulatory Speech Attributes & Training-Free Retrieval (Shahin et al., 2023; Tu et al., 2025)
- **Shahin et al. (UNSW Sydney, 2023 / Speech Communication 2025; P07):** Decomposed phonemes into 35 binary speech attributes (manner and place of articulation: nasal, stop, coronal, voiced, etc.). They introduced Separable CTC with Shared Blank (SCTC-SB) on top of Wav2Vec 2.0. By sharing a single blank token across all 35 attribute heads, the model ensures synchronous temporal emission across articulatory dimensions. Deletions and substitutions were evaluated via sequence-level Levenshtein alignment between decoded attribute streams and target attribute sequences.
- **Tu et al. (HUST / VNPT AI, 2025; P08):** Proposed a training-free retrieval MDD framework. Frame-level representations from a pretrained Wav2Vec2 ASR model are matched against an un-fine-tuned phoneme embedding pool via k-Nearest Neighbors. The resulting frame labels are collapsed via CTC rules ($\mathcal{B}$) and aligned with canonical text via edit distance, achieving a 69.60% F1 score on L2-ARCTIC without any gradient updates.

## 4. Deletion and Omission Modeling: Theoretical Mechanisms & Empirical Failure Audits

### 4.1 The Physics of Deletion: Why Interval Segmentation Fails ($\Delta t = 0$)
In acoustic phonetics, a phoneme omission is fundamentally an **event of acoustic non-realization**. 
When a native speaker pronounces the word *"fast"* (`/F AE S T/`), aerodynamic airflow produces a coronal closure followed by an alveolar burst for `/T/` spanning approximately 40–80 ms. 
When a non-native Vietnamese learner omits the final `/T/` (producing `[f æ s]`), the acoustic duration of `/T/` is strictly:

$$\Delta t_{\text{realized}} = 0\,\text{ms}$$

#### The Forced Alignment Dilemma:
An automated forced aligner (e.g., Montreal Forced Aligner, Kaldi Viterbi aligner) is given the prompt text transcript and constrained to force all canonical phonemes onto the timeline $[0, T_{\text{total}}]$. Faced with an omitted phone, the aligner has only two failure modes:
1. **Boundary Compression:** It squeezes the missing phoneme into a minimum degenerate duration (typically 1–2 frames, 10–20 ms) located in the adjacent silence or coarticulatory vowel transition.
2. **Boundary Dilation:** It stretches the preceding phoneme `/S/` across the silence and places the `/T/` boundary arbitrarily.
In either case, the acoustic features extracted over $[t_s, t_e]$ do not represent the omitted sound; they represent silence or a completely different phoneme.

In L2-ARCTIC, human phonetic annotators marked omissions by identifying the expected temporal transition span where the canonical phone should have occurred (median duration $= 40.0\,\text{ms}$, exactly 2 Wav2Vec2 frames). However, **these gold TextGrid timestamps are strictly unavailable at deployment/test time**.

---

### 4.2 Empirical Failure of Classical GOP and GOPT on Deletions
Because classical GOP evaluates $\frac{1}{|D|} \sum_{t=t_s}^{t_e} \log P(\mathbf{o}_t \mid p) - \dots$, scoring an omission over an artificial forced-alignment interval evaluates background room noise:
- For low-energy unvoiced stops (`/P/`, `/T/`, `/K/`), silence acoustic likelihood is relatively high under unvoiced models, leading classical GOP to assign falsely high scores to deleted stops (severe false accepts).
- In GOPT (Gong et al., 2022), the model completely failed on utterance completeness (PCC = 0.155 on LibriSpeech TDNN-F and 0.011 on PAII-A TDNN-F), because the Kaldi GOP feature pipeline provides no inductive mechanism for missing tokens.

---

### 4.3 The Fallacy of Blank Frame Posteriors: Empirical Audit from RQ1-DIAG-02B
A tempting hypothesis in CTC-based MDD was: *“Since blank ($\epsilon$) denotes non-emission, why not simply measure the mean CTC blank posterior $P(\epsilon \mid \mathbf{h}_t)$ over the expected phoneme interval to detect omissions with >84% confidence?”*

Phase **RQ1-DIAG-02B** (`manifests/RQ1-DIAG-02B/SCIENTIFIC_REPORT.md`, canonical report SHA-256 `38aa543a46d5c2da856e0d73f1887a12285576a205c66fc41c0faa194f8647b6`) subjected this claim to a rigorous counterfactual audit:
- **Case-Control Design:** 66 exact matched pairs across 8 speakers, matching omitted phones with correctly realized phones having identical speaker, canonical phoneme identity, word position, and duration within a 20 ms caliper.
- **Results:**
  - Matched mean $P(\text{blank})$ on omissions: **0.8244**.
  - Matched mean $P(\text{blank})$ on correct controls: **0.7322**.
  - Matched canonical posterior mean: **0.0917** (omissions) vs. **0.1797** (controls).
  - Peak canonical posterior: **0.2859** (omissions) vs. **0.4690** (controls).
  - **Mean Blank ROC-AUC:** **0.653** [95% CI: 0.586, 0.746].
  - **Diagnostic TPR at FPR $\le 15\%$:** only **0.242 (24.2%)**, with a False Negative Rate of **0.758 (75.8%)**.

#### Why Blank Posteriors Fail to Discriminate Omissions:
Connectionist Temporal Classification exhibits severe **peakiness** (Graves et al., 2006). CTC outputs spiky non-blank predictions at sharp 20 ms frame boundaries and defaults to the blank token $\epsilon$ across over **70–90% of all frames**, including unvoiced speech, inter-word silence, and steady-state vowels. Consequently, correctly realized phonemes have high blank posteriors across the majority of their duration. A high blank posterior is an intrinsic property of CTC modeling, **not a reliable signature of phoneme omission**.

### 4.4 Root-Cause Audit of E1 Skip-Token Collapse (RQ-MODEL-01)
In Experiment E1 (`manifests/RQ-MODEL-01/RQ_MODEL_01_REPORT.md`), we introduced an alignment-aware cross-attention mechanism with an explicit acoustic skip token $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$ prepended to $\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}$ and a Gaussian monotonic prior $B_{i, t} = -\gamma (t/T - i/N)^2$.
When evaluated on remote Kaggle GPU (`manifests/RQ-MODEL-01/PROCESS_CHECKPOINT.md`), E1 suffered complete inductive bias collapse:
1. **Skip Mass on Deletions Collapsed to 0.000000:** The mean cross-attention weight on $\mathbf{s}$ for omitted phonemes was identically **0.0%**.
2. **Target Metrics Degraded:**
   - Omission AP dropped from **0.035020** (E0) to **0.032252** (E1) (**-7.90% relative**).
   - Omission Recall @ 15% FPR dropped from **40.00%** to **36.55%** (**-8.62% relative**).
   - Binary AP dropped from **0.317021** to **0.309293** (**-2.44% relative**).

#### Three Root Causes:
1. **Unconstrained Attention Competition:** Queries $\mathbf{q}_i$ for omitted phones found stronger dot-product correlation with diffuse speech frames (vowels, background noise) than with the static, randomly initialized vector $\mathbf{s}$.
2. **Absence of Direct Attention Supervision:** The skip weight was fed into the binary classification head, but no direct loss penalty ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{skip}} = -\log \alpha_{i, \text{skip}}$) was applied to force attention to route mass to $\mathbf{s}$ on deletions. Gradients backpropagated through the binary head were too weak to overcome the acoustic frame bias.
3. **Monotonic Prior Insufficiency:** The diagonal prior enforced chronological progression, but could not resolve the difference between a real phone and silence.

### 4.5 Disentangling the Deletion Triad: Gold Omission vs. CTC Deletion vs. Binary False Negative
A major source of confusion identified in `manifests/RQ1-DIAG-01/checkpoints/01-phoneme-omission-and-false-negative-root-cause.md` is the confounding of three distinct concepts:

```text
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| THE DELETION TRIAD IN MISPRONUNCIATION DETECTION                                                  |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. True Gold Phoneme Omission (Speaker Error):                                                    |
|    - Level: Linguistic / Production ground truth.                                                 |
|    - Definition: Non-native speaker omitted a canonical phoneme (e.g., /T/ in "fast" -> [f ae s]).|
|    - Prevalence in L2-ARCTIC Train: ~3.0% (2,464 tokens/fold); Val: ~1.59% (629 tokens across 4 folds)|
|    - Supervision: Present in canonical prompt tokens, absent from realized acoustic frames.       |
|                                                                                                   |
| 2. CTC Levenshtein Deletion (ASR Acoustic Error):                                                 |
|    - Level: Acoustic Recognition / Decoding failure.                                              |
|    - Definition: The CTC greedy decoder failed to emit an acoustic frame token for a sound that    |
|      the speaker PHYSICALLY UTTERED, causing a gap when aligned against realized target.          |
|    - Macro rate in Baseline R: ~4.9% across folds.                                                |
|                                                                                                   |
| 3. Binary MDD False Negative (Detection Model Failure):                                           |
|    - Level: Classifier Decision Error.                                                            |
|    - Definition: The binary head predicted probability p_hat < tau (predicting "correct") for a   |
|      canonical phoneme that had gold label y = 1 (omission or substitution).                      |
|    - Rate in Baseline R: 97.46% of omissions missed at tau = 0.50; 51.41% missed at tau = 0.20.    |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

#### Crucial Operational Insight:
If a speaker omits a phoneme, that phoneme is **absent from the acoustic stream**.
When an accurate ASR CTC head decodes the speech into recognized phonemes $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}$, the omitted phoneme **will naturally not appear in $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}$**.
Therefore, when $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}$ is aligned against the canonical prompt $\mathbf{Y}^*$ via Levenshtein edit distance, the omitted phoneme will be aligned with the null symbol $\epsilon$—**a string alignment deletion!**
Baseline R failed to detect omissions not because the CTC head couldn't recognize them, but because Baseline R evaluated omissions solely through the **Cross-Attention Binary MLP head**, ignoring the CTC decoding output!

## 5. Available Supervision Signals & Segmentation-Free Formulations

### 5.1 What Supervision Exists Under Blind Inference?
At test and deployment time, the Langfens Speaking web service receives **only two inputs**:
1. Raw audio waveform $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{T_{\text{audio}}}$.
2. Prompt sentence text (e.g., *"He made a quick decision"*).

From these inputs:
- Text is converted deterministically into canonical prompt phonemes $\mathbf{c} = (c_1, \dots, c_N)$ via G2P (`src/langfens_prep/g2p.py`).
- Audio is processed by Wav2Vec2 into latent frames $\mathbf{H} = (\mathbf{h}_1, \dots, \mathbf{h}_T)$.
- Gold TextGrid intervals, boundaries, and rater scores are **strictly inaccessible**.

During training, our authorized fold package (`manifests/RQ1-SPLIT-01/`) provides:
- Realized phoneme target $\mathbf{r} = (r_1, \dots, r_M)$ (which excludes omissions, but includes substitutions, additions, and correct phones).
- Binary error labels $\mathbf{y} \in \{0, 1\}^N$ aligned to the canonical tokens $c_1, \dots, c_N$.
- Substitution diagnosis targets $\mathbf{d} \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{phones}}^N$ (masked to -100 on correct phones and deletions).

---

### 5.2 Sequence-Level Dynamic Programming (Levenshtein) Formulation
Let $\hat{\mathbf{y}} = (\hat{y}_1, \dots, \hat{y}_K) = \mathcal{B}(\arg\max P_{\text{CTC}}(\cdot \mid \mathbf{H}))$ be the greedy decoded phoneme sequence from the CTC head.
Let $\mathbf{c} = (c_1, \dots, c_N)$ be the canonical phoneme prompt sequence.
We compute the minimum edit distance matrix $\mathbf{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+1) \times (K+1)}$:

$$D(i, j) = \min \begin{cases}
D(i-1, j-1) + \text{cost}_{\text{sub}}(c_i, \hat{y}_j) & \text{(Match / Substitution)} \\
D(i-1, j) + \text{cost}_{\text{del}}(c_i) & \text{(Deletion / Omission)} \\
D(i, j-1) + \text{cost}_{\text{ins}}(\hat{y}_j) & \text{(Insertion)}
\end{cases}$$

Where standard costs are $\text{cost}_{\text{match}} = 0$, $\text{cost}_{\text{sub}} = 1$ (if $c_i \neq \hat{y}_j$), $\text{cost}_{\text{del}} = 1$, $\text{cost}_{\text{ins}} = 1$.
Backtracking from $(N, K)$ to $(0, 0)$ yields the optimal alignment path $\mathcal{A} = \{(c_i \to a_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ where $a_i \in \mathcal{V} \cup \{\epsilon\}$.

#### Segmentation-Free MDD Decision Rules:
For each canonical phoneme $c_i$:
1. **Omission (Deletion):** If $a_i = \epsilon$, predict $\hat{y}_{\text{MDD}, i} = 1$, error category = `DELETION`.
2. **Substitution:** If $a_i = s \in \mathcal{V}$ with $s \neq c_i$, predict $\hat{y}_{\text{MDD}, i} = 1$, error category = `SUBSTITUTION`, diagnosis = $s$.
3. **Correct:** If $a_i = c_i$, predict $\hat{y}_{\text{MDD}, i} = 0$, error category = `CORRECT`.

**Core Scientific Advantage:** This formulation is **100% segmentation-free**. It requires no acoustic intervals, no forced alignment, and no frame-level blank heuristics. When a learner omits a sound, the CTC decoder omits the sound from $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$, and dynamic programming maps $c_i \to \epsilon$.

---

### 5.3 CTC-Constrained Viterbi Forward-Backward Alignment Formulation
While greedy decoding + Levenshtein alignment provides a discrete decision, computer-assisted pronunciation training benefits from continuous confidence calibration.
To obtain continuous posterior probabilities for each canonical token $c_i$ without external forced alignment, we can perform **CTC-constrained Viterbi alignment** over the canonical prompt.

Let the canonical sequence with intervening blanks be $\mathbf{c}' = (\epsilon, c_1, \epsilon, c_2, \dots, \epsilon, c_N, \epsilon)$ of length $2N+1$.
The standard CTC forward variable $\alpha_t(s)$ represents the total probability of all prefixes mapping to $\mathbf{c}'_{1:s}$ at frame $t$:

$$\alpha_t(s) = \left[ \alpha_{t-1}(s) + \alpha_{t-1}(s-1) + \mathbb{I}(s) \alpha_{t-1}(s-2) \right] P(c'_s \mid \mathbf{h}_t)$$

Where $\mathbb{I}(s) = 1$ if $c'_s \neq \epsilon$ and $c'_s \neq c'_{s-2}$, and $0$ otherwise.
To model deletions in the canonical trellis, we introduce an explicit **deletion transition** bypassing state $s$ to state $s+2$ with penalty $\lambda_{\text{del}}$:

$$\alpha_t(s) = \left[ \alpha_{t-1}(s) + \alpha_{t-1}(s-1) + \mathbb{I}(s) \alpha_{t-1}(s-2) + \lambda_{\text{del}} \alpha_t(s-2) \right] P(c'_s \mid \mathbf{h}_t)$$

This allows the forward-backward algorithm to assign posterior probability mass to the deletion event without requiring physical acoustic frames.

## 8. Thesis Scope Boundaries & Risk Governance

### 8.1 Compliance with UIT Thesis Proposal (KLTN0127)
The UIT Graduation Thesis Proposal sets precise academic boundaries:
- **Reference-Based Evaluation:** Priority is given to read-aloud practice and prompt-based dialogues where the canonical text is available. General unconstrained open-vocabulary ASR is explicitly out of scope.
- **Phoneme-Level Analysis:** Focus on target phonemic error groups (substitutions, omissions, confusable phoneme pairs).
- **Educational Integration:** Results must feed into tiered explanations, RAG-assisted grammar feedback, and the Langfens Speaking interactive platform.

---

### 8.2 Guarding Against Drift: Foundation ASR vs. Reference-Based MDD
- **Drift Risk:** Drifting into building a general speech-to-text foundation model (e.g., competing with Whisper or LibriSpeech ASR benchmarks).
- **Scope Guard:** The Wav2Vec 2.0 backbone is treated as a **frozen or lightly fine-tuned acoustic feature encoder**. Evaluation is strictly locked to reference-based phoneme detection accuracy (Binary AP, Omission Recall, PER) on L2-ARCTIC.

---

### 8.3 Guarding Against Over-Simplification: The Deletion-Only Bias
- **Risk:** In attempting to resolve the 97.5% omission false-negative rate, implementing aggressive heuristic post-processing (e.g., arbitrary duration clipping or uncalibrated blank thresholds) that inflates false alarms on correct phones and destroys substitution precision.
- **Scope Guard:** All proposed candidates must report both **Omission AP** and **Full Binary AP** simultaneously. An improvement in omission detection is only valid if it maintains or improves overall Binary AP ($>0.317$) and preserves False Positive Rate ceilings ($\text{FPR} \le 15\%$).

---

### 8.4 Division of Research Questions: RQ1 vs. RQ2
- **RQ1 (Speaking & Pronunciation Assessment):** Exclusively owns acoustic feature extraction, CTC decoding, cross-attention alignment, MDD heads, SpeechOcean762 cross-corpus evaluation, and formative pronunciation feedback.
- **RQ2 (Multi-Agent Automated Test Generation):** Exclusively owns reading comprehension question generation, grammar exercise creation, LLM multi-agent coordination (AutoGen/LangGraph), and test quality rubric verification.
- **Strict Boundary:** No multi-agent or LLM prompting logic will be merged into the RQ1 acoustic codebase.

## 9. Candidate Architectural Solutions & Minimal Falsifiable Pilot Experiments

We formulate three candidate architectures within Family A, ordered by implementation complexity and risk profile:

### 9.1 Candidate A.1: Pure CTC ASR Greedy Decoding with Levenshtein MDD Projection
- **Architecture:** Standard Wav2Vec2 + CTC Linear Head (identical to Baseline R CTC head). At test time, bypass the Cross-Attention Binary Head entirely for discrete classification. Run greedy CTC decoding to obtain $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$, execute Levenshtein alignment against canonical $\mathbf{c}$, and project edit operations directly to binary and diagnostic MDD labels.
- **Strengths:** 100% segmentation-free; zero additional parameters; completely immune to attention collapse; naturally maps omissions to deletions.
- **Weakness:** Produces hard binary decisions $\{0, 1\}$ rather than continuous probabilities, limiting threshold tuning unless edit distance scores are smoothed using posterior margins.

### 9.2 Candidate A.2: Dual-Path Hybrid MDD (CTC Alignment + Cross-Attention Scoring)
- **Architecture:** Combine both pathways into an ensemble decision head:
  - Path 1 (CTC + Levenshtein): Detects categorical omissions and additions via sequence alignment. If canonical token $c_i$ is aligned to $\epsilon$, assign high omission prior $p_{\text{dp}, i} = 0.90$.
  - Path 2 (Cross-Attention Binary Head): Evaluates acoustic substitution subtleties on realized phones.
  - Final Prediction: $p_{\text{final}, i} = \max(p_{\text{binary}, i}, p_{\text{dp}, i})$.
- **Strengths:** Directly solves the omission false-negative bottleneck while retaining continuous probability calibration for substitutions and correct phones.
- **Weakness:** Requires careful probability calibration to prevent false-alarm inflation.

### 9.3 Candidate A.3: CTC-Constrained Trellis Forced Scoring with Explicit Deletion Arc
- **Architecture:** Replace unconstrained cross-attention with a differentiable CTC Viterbi trellis conditioned on the canonical prompt sequence $\mathbf{c}'$. Add an explicit skip transition arc between blank states with a learnable deletion transition logit $\theta_{\text{del}}$.
- **Strengths:** Mathematically unified, fully differentiable, segmentation-free.
- **Weakness:** Higher engineering complexity; requires custom GPU Viterbi kernel or log-space dynamic programming.
