# Lane C — Deletion-Specific Supervised Objectives

**Disposition: GO for research/pilot consideration only; not execution authorization.** A minimal two-head-compatible candidate is a deletion-supervised null/skip loss on the existing alignment-aware binary branch. Its rationale is that the existing skip token received no direct deletion supervision and collapsed in E1; its effectiveness remains speculative. Keep Baseline R and all existing training contracts unchanged until a separately authorized, separately configured pilot.

### Supervision and identifiability

CTC’s blank is a frame-level alignment symbol, not a canonical-token “omitted” class. It appears in valid paths for correctly spoken phones, including between repeated labels, closures, and transitions. Here, deletion phones are removed from the realized CTC transcript (`dataset.py:105–118`), so CTC has no explicit canonical deletion target. A high blank posterior cannot by itself identify a true omission.

Canonical prompt phone and error label are available in training; a realized phone exists for correct and substitution tokens but not deleted phones. Gold TextGrid **timestamps** are supervision-only metadata; they must not become inference inputs. Any objective using omission labels should test whether the model learns acoustic absence rather than speaker/L1, canonical-phone, word-position, or deletion-prior shortcuts. Confidence should be evaluated on held-out validation speakers and calibrated only on validation—not on train outcomes or test data. Short-phone boundaries are especially uncertain: historical local audit reports describe omission spans near a few Wav2Vec2 frames. Even an annotated “deletion interval” is a reference annotation, not a direct measurement of the physical duration of an absent phone.

## 3. Competing feasible methods

All costs below are **inference/compute expectations, not measurements**; full train-time costs remain unmeasured. Current two-head contract means CTC plus one binary MDD head.

| Method | Substitution vs deletion handling | Supervision actually available / feedback | Integration and principal risks | Relative cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Hierarchical or 3-way correct/substitution/deletion classification** | Explicitly separates deletion from substitution; can output \(P(\text{sub})\), \(P(\text{del})\). | TextGrid has correct/sub/del labels; realized phone only supports substitution diagnosis. Deletion label can support “possible omission,” not the identity of an absent sound. | Replace binary output/loss with 3 logits; violates present binary-head contract and requires a new configuration/checkpoint. Rare deletion class; softmax competition and phone/canonical priors may dominate. | Similar inference, marginally larger head; training comparable, unmeasured. Expanded objective authorization required. |
| **B. Binary error head plus supervised skip/null mass (recommended minimal pilot)** | Retains binary error detector; deletion-specific auxiliary loss teaches a null/skip signal distinct from substitution. | Deletion-vs-nondeletion label available; realized-phone auxiliary target masked for deletion. Feedback could distinguish “possible omitted target” from generic error, but cannot name a realized replacement for deletion. | Use E1 skip weights with an auxiliary BCE/soft target on the existing attention skip mass; keep CTC + binary heads. Tests direct identifiability of the failed E1 mechanism. Attention mass can be forced by label without truly grounding absence; attention probabilities may be diffuse or multi-head ambiguous. | No extra inference head, only E1’s extra key and a scalar; auxiliary loss adds training compute/memory. Unmeasured. |
| **C. Deletion-vs-realized-phone contrastive objective** | Separates missing target from realized phone evidence; can use CTC frame posteriors/features for canonical-phone vs blank/noncanonical contrast. | Labels give deleted canonical phone and absence label; CTC gives frame evidence, but blank is ambiguous and no token alignment is available without timestamp leakage. | New loss over aligned/token-conditioned frame representations or batch pairs; high matching and calibration burden. False “absence” on closures and short transitions; potential shortcut to duration/context. | Extra training similarity/loss computation; inference potentially extra score only. Unmeasured. |
| **D. Synthetic deletion augmentation** | Could add targeted deletion positives while preserving canonical reference; substitutions remain separate labels. | Train labels plus timing can generate masked/cut segments; synthetic waveform is not natural pronunciation evidence. | Augment only train, no gold boundaries at inference. Risks artificial cut edges, lost coarticulation, silent-window shortcuts, mismatch to natural Vietnamese codas. | More data/encoder forwards and storage; no product head required. Cost and benefit unmeasured. |
| **E. Existing BCE/focal/weighted objective** | All errors share one positive class; no deletion-specific diagnosis. | Binary labels only. Feedback is generic “mispronunciation,” with no reliable omission subtype. | Already integrated. Prior Fold 1 focal and weighted pilots did not beat Baseline R at fixed budget. Threshold shifts and ranking/calibration must be separated. | Lowest implementation cost; training already archived. Not a deletion solution. |

A hierarchical pipeline can preserve an overall error score while decomposing subtype probabilities, but hierarchy does not solve the acoustic evidence problem by itself. Likewise, a binary head with an auxiliary realized-phone classifier cannot diagnose deletion from its realized-phone target because that target is deliberately masked on deletions.

## 5. Candidate shortlist and minimal falsifiable pilot

**Shortlist:** (1) deletion-supervised skip/null auxiliary loss with existing E1 skip mechanism; (2) 3-class MDD as a separately authorized architecture comparison if direct skip supervision fails; (3) contrastive acoustic evidence only after establishing a leakage-free, non-oracle token-to-frame sampling rule. Synthetic augmentation is lower priority because of cut-artifact risk and no direct evidence.

**Smallest two-head-compatible proposal (INFERENCE/PROPOSAL):** Keep the CTC head and binary MDD output. Add a training-only loss on existing E1 skip weights:
\[
L = L_{\rm CTC} + L_{\rm binary} + \lambda L_{\rm skip},\quad
L_{\rm skip}=\operatorname{BCE}(\alpha_{\rm skip},\,1[y=\text{deletion}]).
\]
Use valid canonical-token masks; no TextGrid timestamps as model inputs. This tests the direct-supervision distinction from E1 (which had no skip-mass loss). Its main falsifier is that skip mass separates deletion from nondeletion on held-out speakers **and** omission detection improves at a matched validation FPR without overall MDD deterioration. Attention-supervision is not synonymous with causal acoustic evidence; evaluate audio dependence and calibration separately.

**Pilot design, only after separate operator authorization:** Compare unchanged Baseline R (archived) against a fresh, independently initialized E1-supervised arm, both using the same frozen `facebook/wav2vec2-base-960h` revision, Fold 1 train/validation whitelist, seed/optimizer/schedule, and 3,600 successful-update fixed-budget endpoint. Do not warm-start E1, use test speakers, or change the baseline contract. Validation-selected best is secondary; fixed-budget is primary. Track updates/skips and checkpoint provenance. Use validation only for early stopping/threshold selection and disclose reuse; no threshold fitted on the evaluation endpoint should be presented as independent calibration.

Pre-register: (i) omission AP on all 145 Fold 1 validation deletions; (ii) omission recall at a threshold whose correct-token FPR is at most 15%, with uncertainty; (iii) overall binary AP and CTC PER guardrails; (iv) deletion-vs-correct and deletion-vs-substitution calibration/confusion; (v) skip-mass distributions by error type, phone, duration bin and speaker; (vi) matched/mismatched acoustic control or equivalent input perturbation. Report per-speaker outcomes: Fold 1 has only two validation speakers, limiting generalization. A null result, no skip separation, or gains only via higher FPR falsifies the pilot rationale. Success would justify a later four-fold, train/validation-only proposal—not deployment or test evaluation.

Equal weighted scores for the leading ideas conceal different risks: skip supervision is the smallest architectural delta but weakly grounded; 3-class labels are explicit but break the two-head/binary contract. Uncertainty is high: none has a verified Langfens deletion-specific win, and the two-speaker validation set cannot resolve cross-speaker robustness.

**Integration seam:** product Speaking currently receives/transcribes audio, but `services/ai-service/app/prompts/speaking_grade.py` explicitly says pronunciation scoring is transcript-based and prohibits claims about phonemes or sound quality. `services/speaking-service/AGENTS.md` describes Whisper transcription and AI grading. A deployed acoustic MDD feature therefore needs a real audio/reference-phoneme scoring interface and confidence-aware feedback contract; the current LLM transcript prompt is not that capability. No product files were changed.

**Residual risks:** deletion labels may encourage canonical-phone, word-position, or L1 priors; class imbalance and validation prevalence shift undermine calibration; CTC blank is not an omission label; short-phone/boundary uncertainty weakens acoustic localization; synthetic cuts create shortcuts; G=2 Fold 1 validation speakers are inadequate for general claims. DIAG-02B’s archived parity failures also counsel against causal claims. No training, model loading, audio/label reading, sealed-test access, backward pass, optimizer update, or fresh experiment was performed in this research task.
