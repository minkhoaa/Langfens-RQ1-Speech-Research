## 3. Cao et al.: exact naming, equations, comparisons and transfer limits

### 3.1 Naming and mathematical distinctions

The paper's family is **GOP-SF** (“segmentation-free”), with complete result names such as **GOP-CTC-SF-S**, **GOP-CTC-SF-SD**, and **GOP-CTC-SF-SDI**. “GOP-S/SD/SDI” is shorthand for the alternatives allowed at the target position, not three separate papers and not a reason to drop the SF/model qualifier. **GOP-SA** means self-alignment, a different method (§III-A): use model-matched activation alignment rather than an external segmenter. The authors expressly distinguish finding model activations from locating the physical acoustic segment.

Let `L_C = (L_L, l_i, L_R)` be the canonical phone sequence and `O` the utterance. Standard CTC (Eq.4) computes

`P_CTC(L | O) = sum_{pi: B(pi)=L} product_t y_t(pi_t)`.

Removing CTC blanks/repeats still leaves **every required label of the chosen target sequence**. An ordinary forced canonical CTC alignment does not acquire a canonical-token DELETE operation just because it contains blank frames.

Cao's Eq.5 defines `GOP-SF(l_i) = log p(l_i | O, L_L, L_R)`. Their derivation marginalizes possible target alignments and assumes a uniform alignment prior that cancels in Eq.10. The denominator is **not merely a blank score, unrelated utterance, free-phone best path, or raw canonical CTC loss**. It is a target-relaxed graph with fixed left/right canonical context (§III-C):

- **S:** `L_L . L_R` — exactly one arbitrary phone replaces the target;
- **SD:** `L_L .? L_R` — one arbitrary phone **or empty**;
- **SDI:** `L_L .* L_R` — arbitrary phone sequence, including empty.

Figure 3's graph transitions distinguish those alternatives. Eq.12 is the loss difference

`GOP-SF(l_i) = L_CTC(L_relaxed, O) - L_CTC(L_C, O)`

with the graph CTC loss corresponding to the chosen S/SD/SDI variant. Thus **the competing denominator is central to the method**. Renaming a forced-alignment duration or a single target-deleted CTC score “GOP-SF-SD” would misdescribe it. A deletion-only versus canonical likelihood contrast is a legitimate *different* hypothesis; it needs explicit normalization, priors/calibration and length-effect controls.

Eq.6 additionally divides the log score by an estimated activation occupancy. Eq.13 estimates occupancy by summing normalized forward mass over central wildcard states; the paper floors this normalization quantity at **1**, since deletion can drive occupancy towards zero. This is model-activation occupancy, **not a gold TextGrid duration**. “Numerical” means scaled/normalized forward variables addressing underflow, whereas “Norm” refers to activation-length normalization. Feature vectors FGOP-SF contain canonical log posterior and substitution/deletion log-ratio components (Eqs.14–16); insertions are excluded from that finite feature vector because arbitrarily long alternatives would give infinitely many components.

**Interpretation limit:** these are target-conditioned pronunciation compatibility scores under specified graphs. Allowing an empty alternative is not the same as estimating a calibrated posterior that a human-labelled omission occurred, and segmentation-free scoring does not itself identify a physical time interval for an absent phone. Fixed context also means real errors elsewhere can influence a target score.

### 3.2 What Tables I–III actually establish

**CMU Kids (§IV-A):** 150,899 labelled phones, with 90.2% correct, 3.2% substitution, 2.3% deletion and 4.3% insertion in the authors' derived corpus distribution. “Real errors” come from comparing corpus phonetic transcripts with dictionary canonical pronunciations **for utterances marked problematic**; all canonical phones in other utterances are labelled correct. These are derived labels, not a new manually adjudicated L2-ARCTIC omission test. “Simulated errors” systematically substitute canonical symbols while keeping recorded speech unchanged; they do not simulate acoustic deletion.

**Table I: macro-by-phone AUC-ROC**, not omission AP, omission recall, or error diagnosis accuracy:

| Method | Simulated substitutions AUC | Mixed real errors AUC |
|---|---:|---:|
| GOP-DNN-Avg | 0.824 | 0.796 |
| GOP-CE-Avg | 0.967 | 0.851 |
| GOP-CTC-SA | 0.988 | 0.905 |
| GOP-CTC-SF-S | 0.989 | 0.891 |
| GOP-CTC-SF-SD | 0.986 | **0.914** |
| GOP-CTC-SF-SDI | 0.938 | 0.859 |

The paper supplies 95% CIs for each entry. SD does better on mixed real errors than S; S is stronger on the synthetic substitution task. **More permissive is not automatically better:** SDI is worse here. §V-A explains that the canonical-token evaluation only reflects insertions through effects on neighboring canonical phones; it is not a full insertion-detection assessment. There is **no isolated omission AP/result** from which a Langfens target can be borrowed.

**Table II** varies acoustic-model peakiness and scoring definitions. For example, standard CTC has PER 11.46%, but its GOP-Avg/SA/SF AUC values differ (0.824/0.909/0.914). This supports separating recognition accuracy, peakiness and scoring quality. It does not prove blank mass alone diagnoses omission. The table describes a separate checkpoint-controlled experiment; its SA value should not silently replace Table I's 0.905.

**SpeechOcean762 (§IV-A,D and Table III)** uses ordinal phone scores 0/1/2 and **PCC**, not explicit natural-deletion labels with the Langfens omission denominator. Published examples: scalar GOP-CTC-SF-SD-numerical + polynomial regression **0.450 ± 0.006** PCC; FGOP-CTC-SF-Norm + SVR **0.581 ± 0.006**; the same normalized feature family + GOPT **0.648 ± 0.002**. Those last results include a trained supervised downstream assessor; they are not pure threshold-free raw GOP. GOPT results are averaged over five initialization runs with selected models (§IV-E). The authors acknowledge another method at PCC 0.693 (§V-D); “state of the art” here must preserve their **assessor-held-constant** comparison, not universal superiority.

**Model/data mismatch:** the modern acoustic models use `wav2vec2.0-large-xlsr-53`, fine-tuned on LibriSpeech train-clean-100 with the feature extractor frozen (§IV-C). That is not the same as a wholly frozen `facebook/wav2vec2-base-960h` backbone and Langfens's trained phone head. CMU child speech and SpeechOcean ordinal assessment are not the frozen Langfens speaker-disjoint folds.

## 4. GECToR: verified original, unvalidated speech transfer

P2 is a text-to-text grammatical error correction study. §§3–5 map source tokens to token transformations; a pretrained **text** Transformer feeds error detection and tagging projections, with iterative application to the modified sentence. The default edit inventory has 5,000 tags including KEEP, DELETE, APPEND, REPLACE and grammatical transformations. It is not originally a 41-class phone realization model.

Table 7 reports XLNet single-model F0.5 **65.3** on CoNLL-2014 and **72.4** on BEA-2019; these are grammatical-edit benchmarks, not MDD. Table 8's speed comparison is specifically **Tesla V100, batch size 128, CoNLL-2014**, with 0.40 seconds for five tagger iterations versus 4.35 seconds for the beam-12 Transformer-NMT comparison. It does not supply single-utterance audio latency on a T4 or CPU. The current official README warns that later-library checkpoint scores differ from the paper, and links the older reproduction revision; provenance matters even for the original text task.

At paper-reproduction revision `fea1532608bf50d086c0b62cb9c4fdc3e5c71daa`, `gector/seq2labels_model.py:79–85,130–134` shows the text embedder and tag/error projections. At current inspected revision, `gector/gec_model.py:127–147` maps a `$DELETE` edit to an empty **text replacement**. Neither is an acoustic omission detector.

A speech adaptation can reasonably borrow the **structured label idea**, but must supply and validate its own acoustic representation, canonical-position grounding, realized-phone/edit annotation policy, repeated-phone alignment, class imbalance treatment and confidence calibration. The direction of editing must be stated: canonical→realized makes an omitted expected phone DELETE; correcting erroneous realized text back to canonical can require the opposite operation. A canonical-only 41-class head also does not represent arbitrary inserted speech without additional inter-token slots/decoding rules. Even KEEP versus REPLACE(canonical-phone) needs a consistent redundancy policy.

**Actionable classification:** call it “proposed canonical-conditioned supervised phone/edit tagging,” not a published GECToR-Speech result. Benchmark against a simple subtype classifier under matched capacity/data before crediting sequence-tagging novelty. Explicit DELETE labels provide a supervised target, but a model can still learn phoneme/position/speaker priors and ignore audio; require acoustic and canonical-only controls. No convergence within 3,000/3,600 updates, omission superiority, 3.6-GB footprint, or 14.2/42.5-ms inference guarantee is supported by P2.

## 5. Speech-native families: what is established and what is not

### 5.1 Alignment and acoustic scoring

**CTC/forced alignment (P11, P1):** standard CTC sums paths constrained to a specified output string. A canonical forced aligner can allocate model activation to an expected-but-absent phone rather than report a labelled omission. Short duration and blank are possible features, not proof. CTC blank is a non-emission path symbol, **not equivalent to silence, a negative articulatory attribute, or omitted-phone ground truth**. Conversely, ordinary forced alignment is not universally unusable: it remains a legitimate boundary-estimation comparator with disclosed errors. No universal omitted-phone duration cutoff is established by these reads. Inference must use waveform/reference-derived alignments, never gold TextGrid times.

**Chen et al. (P6)** is a concrete speech alternative: eight articulatory dimensions, frame-to-canonical similarity, forward-only chunks of **three** canonical phones, similarity threshold **0.8**, and unknown/zero padding if no suitable candidate is present (§2.2). Their design criteria explicitly include skipping a missed canonical phone and avoiding matching an inserted speech frame to an ordinary reference phone. Table 2 gives aggregate F1 **58.79%** versus CTC-ATT **56.2%**, with recall/precision changing from **46.57/70.28** to **65.38/53.41**. It is evidence for a speech-specific alignment design and a trade-off, not calibrated probabilistic epsilon-deletion inference, guaranteed acoustic localization or omission AP. Different corpora/training and reported aggregate endpoints prevent direct Langfens ranking.

**MoChA (P12)** is Chiu & Raffel, ICLR 2018, arXiv **1712.05382v2**. Raffel et al.'s ICML 2017 *Online and Linear-Time Attention by Enforcing Monotonic Alignments*, arXiv **1704.00784**, is its distinct predecessor, not the same title/source. P12 explicitly targets online and linear-time test decoding through hard-monotonic chunk selection and local soft attention. Rejecting the whole family as inherently too slow contradicts that stated objective. Neither monotonicity nor chunk attention alone supplies an omitted-*reference*-phone state; its orientation and edit semantics must be designed. The 2017 predecessor was identified in P12, not independently retrieved here.

**Joint-Align:** M1's full primary text was blocked. Stop there. Historical soft-DTW/VRAM claims are not admitted. Tu's Table 1 quotes Joint-Align F1 63.04, precision 77.12 and recall 53.31; those quoted figures do not support the incompatible precision/recall pair in the old lane E table, and do not substitute for original-paper verification.

### 5.2 Supervised and reference-aware speech models

**Fu et al. (P7)** uses audio queries attending to canonical-phone keys/values, concatenated acoustic residual, and realized-phone CTC (§2, Eqs.7–10). That is a genuine joint speech/reference precedent, with a different attention orientation from a canonical-query phone classifier. Table 3: baseline aggregate F1 **49.29**, best VC10% **56.08**, while error recall decreases **74.78→56.12%**. Table 4: correctly diagnosed deletions **499/918→482/918**, not an omission-diagnosis improvement. Overall F1 and fewer diagnosis errors do not establish better coverage of omissions. Prompt perturbations create training mismatches without necessarily creating physically omitted phones. Also, the paper describes ordinary dot-product attention as monotonic; the written softmax alone is not proof of a hard monotonic constraint. Author terminology and guaranteed mechanism must be distinguished.

**Zhang et al. (P8)** directly conditions error-state prediction on audio and target phones: Eq.1 binary error BCE, Eq.2 optional realized-phone CE, Eq.3 joint loss. Its augmentation replaces clustered SSL acoustic units, uses unit-vector distance as a synthetic error indicator, and trains an MSE distance objective before natural-label fine-tuning (Eqs.7–8). Table 3 at full labelled-data fraction: aggregate F1 **0.589** for the direct model, **0.596** with text augmentation, **0.606** with proposed augmentation. This is better speech-specific motivation for supervised acoustic tagging than GECToR alone, but not a validated natural-deletion subtype objective. It uses an **8:1:1** annotated-utterance split, LibriSpeech and unlabeled L2 speech, and MFA-derived augmentation aggregation; it is not the Langfens speaker-fold/budget contract. It must not authorize cross-partition use of unlabeled speech in this project.

**Wu et al. (P9)** shows legitimate AED and wav2vec2-CTC free-phone models on **CU-CHLOE**. Table 2 reports T-1 F1 **77.23%**, T-2 **80.98%**; T-2 PER **5.97%** is not an L2-ARCTIC result. The actual T-2 setup uses **20,000 updates**, freezing CNN throughout and Transformer only during the first 10,000 (§3.2.2). Reproducing that setup violates the present fully frozen-backbone/3,600-update candidate constraint, but does **not** prove every compact AED/transducer needs those resources or diverges on small data.

**RefTextLAS (P10)** supplies strong evidence of reference bias and a poor direct omission benchmark, for specific documented reasons:

- Audio encoder: **five LSTM layers**; bias-token encoder: BiLSTM; decoder: **two LSTM layers**, **4,000 wordpieces** (§3.1). Not a Conformer or phoneme-output system.
- Training: **60,000 hours private far-field speech**, **80 epochs / 400K steps**, not 1,000-hour LibriSpeech. This is a published setup, not a universal minimum resource requirement.
- Evaluation: private **1,417-utterance** reading dataset, token FRR/FAR relative to a hybrid reading system. §3.2 expressly does **not** count repetitions or reading a sentence partially (user deletions) as reading mistakes. No 58.4% phoneme-MDD F1 is established.
- Table 2 E5: relative FRR **−65.0%** but relative FAR **+162.0%**. Table 5 ensemble at Δ=0: **−42.7% / +26.4%**. These are *relative changes*, not absolute rates or phoneme-omission recall. They empirically motivate controls against reference copying, not blanket rejection of all reference-aware approaches.

### 5.3 Phonological/articulatory approaches

P4 uses **35 non-mutually-exclusive attributes**, binary positive/negative streams plus a shared CTC blank, wav2vec2-large-robust, and fine-tuning beyond the feature extractor. It maps canonical phones to reference attribute sequences and aligns each recognized stream to identify missing/inserted/replaced attributes (§2). Attribute “negative” means a phone lacks that particular feature, **not the phone was omitted**.

P5 §3 explains shared blank as **increasing the likelihood** that attribute categories emit blank together. It does not mathematically force identical temporal boundaries for all attribute CTC paths. ## 6. Retrieval: metric reconciliation, rejection semantics and realistic scope

P3 explicitly builds a labelled **training** phone embedding pool using time-aligned phone annotations (§2.1). “Without model training” means no additional MDD-specific parameter training, **not label-free, data-free or compute-free**. The experiment uses a pretrained ASR-fine-tuned **HuBERT-large** model, 500 sampled training utterances, middle-frame pool representation, top-k **10**, cosine threshold **0.7** (§3.2). The cited model identifier is `facebook/hubert-large-ls960-ft`; its exact revision/license was not separately audited here. It is not Langfens's smaller frozen backbone.

Eqs.3–6: retrieve nearest frames, retain neighbors above threshold, assign their modal label; if none survive, assign **blank**. Collapse consecutive duplicates and remove blanks, then align the resulting realized-phone sequence to canonical phones. This has two crucial consequences:

- **Reject/no-neighbor ≠ explicit acoustic omission diagnosis or pedagogical abstention.** It maps a frame to a non-emission symbol, and blank is removed downstream. A user-facing reject/retake policy would be an added design needing calibration.
- An omitted reference phone lacks its own positive query, but an acoustic realization sequence can have a **gap relative to canonical**, which is exactly what sequence comparison can expose. Therefore absence of an omitted-phone frame is not an impossibility proof for retrieval-based omission detection. Recognition failures/insertions can still make this unreliable; deletion-specific evaluation is required.

**Table 1:** F1 **69.60%**, precision **71.78%**, recall **67.56%**, FAR **32.44%**, FRR **4.43%**, DER **37.77%**, PER **104.08%**, correctness **90.42%**. PER `(S+D+I)/N` can exceed 100% when insertions are numerous. Correctness excludes insertions, and canonical-token MDD primarily evaluates whether expected phones match (§3.4.1). Thus these numbers are compatible with poor unconstrained sequence fidelity and reasonable canonical-token detection under that protocol. Neither result is a 104% performance improvement, and no fraud/invalidity follows arithmetically. Conversely, a good F1 does not erase insertion failure or demonstrate good diagnosis: DER is worse than several quoted baselines. Some baselines were copied from publications rather than rerun (§3.4.1), limiting causal superiority claims.

**Table 3** also prevents cherry-picking: top-k=5 reports F1 **71.69%** but PER **135.27%**; all-frame pooling has PER **76.17%**, F1 **62.90%**; a 1,800-utterance pool gives PER **84.63%**, F1 **69.40%**. The method is not uniformly stuck at exactly 104.08% PER, nor does optimizing PER necessarily optimize their MDD F1. Threshold changes exhibit trade-offs, not proof of calibrated uncertainty.

The paper's six-speaker test convention and 12-speaker pool-building partition differ from the Langfens folds (e.g., NJS is a test speaker there but validation in Langfens Fold 1). There is no direct portable absolute ranking. A project-specific pool must be fold-train-only with provenance and consent/license checks; no active TEST annotations or embeddings may enter its index. Reference-aware retrieval fusion is a reasonable **unmeasured hypothesis**, not an established omission solution.
