# METHOD-BENCH-01 External Research (discovery snapshot)

**Search date:** 2026-11-08. This is a discovery synthesis, not evidence of LANGFENS performance. Recent papers' published metrics use different languages, populations, event definitions and operating points; they cannot be ranked against LANGFENS AP without local reproduction.

## Discovery judgment

The most interesting genuinely different formulation in this sweep is **verbatim phone recognition with similarity-aware soft supervision**: separately estimate realized phones and compare against the supplied canonical sequence. It replaces a direct binary/diagnosis head with a structured recognition-plus-edit-diagnosis formulation and can potentially unify S, O, and extra-phone I. A second discovery is weakly supervised anomaly learning (SylPh), where normal pronunciation is modeled and errors are outliers. Both require event-level mapping to be demonstrated; neither can be credited with exact-gap insertion performance from its published task metrics.

2026 evidence found is limited and heterogeneous. Several 2026 search hits are preprints or challenge reports, not yet peer-reviewed final papers. The ICECET paper is an IEEE-indexed conference paper but its full method/code could not be verified from public record in this pass. The OpenPA record is an October 2026 preprint with a public code link. No claim here depends on search-result snippets as proof of LANGFENS improvement.

## Adjacent / follow-up search directions

Additional inspection of the 2026 ISCA proceedings/arXiv found two valuable new candidate families. **Domain-Aware Mispronunciation Detection and Diagnosis Using Language-Specific Statistical Graphs**, Huu Tuong Tu et al., arXiv:2606.05569 / Interspeech 2026, proposes directed phoneme confusion graphs and an L1-specific strategy on L2-ARCTIC; abstract reports 59.52% F1. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05569), [code](https://github.com/huutuongtu/MDD-LSSG). This could structure substitution alternatives, but may mostly encode L1 priors and has no clear exact-gap insertion formulation. **Light-weight Pronunciation Assessment via Discrete Speech Token Surprisal**, Syeda Faiza Ahmed Sara & Shammur Absar Chowdhury, arXiv:2606.19910 / Interspeech 2026, uses SSL discrete units, a native-speech token LM, token surprisal, and transcript-guided Text2DUnit-DTW; abstract reports utterance PCC 0.60→0.66 with transcript guidance and cross-dataset L2-ARCTIC gains. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19910). This is promising low-supervision acoustic anomaly evidence and potentially cheap if codebook/tokens can be extracted, but PCC is not phoneme event AP. A third proceedings lead is *A Fusion-Aware Two-Stage Framework for Mispronunciation Detection and Diagnosis in Low-Resource Modern Standard Arabic* (Jing Yang et al., Interspeech 2026); full method/metrics require review before selection. Full records are in the paper database.

Search also identified *PhonLLM: Joint Phone Recognition and Phonological Process Inference for Child Speech* in Interspeech 2026 program. This is potentially relevant structured recognition plus process interpretation, but different population and requires original-paper inspection. Another potentially transferable family is phoneme-similarity-aware recognition using Sylber representations; inspect the released VCTK-accent data/code before implementation.
