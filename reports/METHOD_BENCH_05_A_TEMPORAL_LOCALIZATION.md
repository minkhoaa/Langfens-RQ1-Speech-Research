## Measured collision diagnostic

For each utterance, I enumerated each of the N+1 gaps and each of E4's eight insertion phones. A gap was marked collision-participating if at least one candidate collapsed phone sequence was also generated by a different gap. Thus this is an event-level flag for at least one colliding alternative, not a claim that every candidate at that gap is ambiguous. “Collision excess” is the sum over duplicated sequence keys of (number of source gaps minus one).

| Population | Gaps | Gold I positives | Collision-participating gaps | Positives in those gaps | Collision excess | E4 AP-I identifiable (support) | E4 AP-I collision-participating (support) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| TRAIN HJK/HKK saved holdout, seed 101 | 10,139 | 46 | 6,795 (67.0%) | 31 (67.4%) | 4,126 | 0.0992 (15/3,344) | 0.0169 (31/6,795) |
| DEV_CAL, seed 101 | 10,343 | 102 | 6,883 (66.6%) | 68 (66.7%) | 4,208 | 0.0407 (34/3,460) | 0.0282 (68/6,883) |

The E4 AP-I overall on these exact saved score populations is 0.03668 on the TRAIN HJK/HKK holdout and 0.0372 on DEV_CAL. Subset APs have very small positive supports and are prevalence-dependent. The collision-participating positive fraction is close to its event fraction in both cohorts; the audit does not show that collisions explain most insertion failures. It does establish that many individual gap alternatives share sequence-likelihood evidence with another gap hypothesis, so sequence likelihood alone cannot attribute that shared evidence to a unique gap.

Repeated adjacent same-phone runs provide the clearest structural example: inserting that same phone at different points inside a run yields identical collapsed sequences. The observed 21 such adjacent target pairs per population are only a narrow diagnostic; the broader collision flag also captures repeated candidate phones across longer spans in a reference.

## Interpretation

**Measured:** sequence-level E4 scoring is underdetermined for a substantial fraction of gap events under the top-eight candidate set. On these saved cohorts, E4 ranks the collision-participating subset less well than the identifiable subset, but the positive supports are small and the result is not a speaker-grouped OOF estimate.

**Inference:** a frame-level or alignment-conditioned score could add location evidence for a candidate phone even where its full collapsed sequence is shared. This needs a controlled comparison; the current audit does not demonstrate that such evidence improves AP-I.

## Temporal scoring intervention design

The matched-control design froze the recognizer, posterior arrays, candidate set (for T0/T1/T2), and complete exact-gap event population. T2 used a predicted reference-conditioned Viterbi alignment and a fixed local posterior-versus-blank window; it did not use gold timing. The experiment preserves all N+1 gap identities, and reports supports/AP separately for collision-participating and identifiable subsets. A forward-backward uncertainty-marginalized T3 was attempted separately but produced no valid saved score; it is documented as unsuccessful below.

## Matched temporal experiment (T0/T1/T2)

A CPU experiment was subsequently run on the identical 300-utterance HJK/HKK saved TRAIN speaker-holdout population (10,139 gaps; 46 positives), using the existing `train_speaker_holdout_posteriors.npz` and E4/E3 score files. No model was refit.

- **T0 E4 canonical mean aggregation:** existing CTC alternative-sequence likelihood ratio; AP-I **0.038718**. The matched max-aggregation variant is also reported for the candidate-set ablation: AP-I **0.036681**.
- **T1 E3:** existing E3 event score on the same posteriors/events; AP-I **0.009966**.
- **T2 temporal:** Viterbi-align the intended phone sequence under the same CTC frame posteriors; place a gap at the midpoint between neighboring aligned phone-state spans (first/last phone boundary for edge gaps); over a fixed ±2-frame window score the maximum candidate-phone log posterior minus blank log posterior among E4's same eight TRAIN-fit insertion candidates. AP-I **0.004263**.

All methods share support of 46/10,139. The Viterbi alignments yielded no adjacent phone-state boundary ties in this population. T2 did not improve over either canonical mean E4, max E4, or E3. It is a deliberately frozen-posterior diagnostic, not a learned temporal event model. It tests only candidate phone occupancy near a single forced-alignment boundary; it does not marginalize uncertainty in gap location, model inserted spans, or account for candidate-duration likelihood. Therefore it is evidence against this specific local-max mechanism, not against all temporal localization methods.

Reproduction: `.venv/bin/python experiments/rq1_method_bench_05/agent_a/temporal_gap_score.py` (CPU, ~4 s observed wall time). It writes per-event T2 scores to `experiments/rq1_method_bench_05/agent_a/temporal_scores.jsonl`; implementation and exact scoring window are in that script. T0/T1 outputs are read-only METHOD-BENCH-04 files. AP is raw ranking AP, not thresholded F1.

### Updated conclusion

The exact-gap collision mechanism is real in candidate-sequence space and many positive events are in collision-participating gaps, but the present subset analyses do not establish causality. The first temporal control failed: local posterior evidence at an intended-reference Viterbi boundary ranked insertions worse than sequence-level E4 on this same small speaker holdout. Follow-up should test uncertainty-marginalized boundary occupancy or a learned temporal head under the grouped folds, using no gold timing.

## Candidate-specific ambiguity and saved PR curves

To distinguish a gap that has *some* colliding alternative from a positive event whose annotated inserted phone itself has a colliding sequence, I also audited insertion-phone coverage on the same HJK/HKK holdout. Gold inserted phones were used only for this post-hoc diagnostic, never as inference features.

| Candidate inventory | Candidate symbols | Gold insertions | Gold phone covered | Positive exact gold-hypothesis collisions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| E4 top-eight TRAIN-derived | 8 | 46 | 23 (50.0%) | 2/46 |
| Full nonblank CTC tokenizer inventory | 42 | 46 | 46 (100%) | 2/46 |

Thus the high 67% collision-participating-gap rate is a broad event flag and overstates how often the *gold inserted-phone hypothesis* is itself ambiguous. The audit distinguishes (a) any candidate at a gap shares its collapsed sequence with another gap (6,795/10,139 gaps and 31/46 positive events among those gaps for top-8), and (b) the annotated gold inserted-phone sequence itself maps to multiple gaps (2/46 positives for either candidate set). Candidate coverage is separate: the top-eight recipe covers only half of gold insertion phones; full-tokenizer coverage is 46/46.

### A4 candidate-set comparison (same frozen posterior/events)

I reran E4 insertion scoring with max aggregation and the same canonical CTC likelihood, changing only the candidate set: the TRAIN-fit top eight versus all 42 nonblank tokenizer symbols. This is a diagnostic on the fixed 10,139 gaps (46 positives), not a candidate set selected for deployment. Gold inserted-phone labels were not used in scoring; they appear only in post-hoc coverage/collision counts.

| Candidate set | Candidate sequences scored | AP-I (max aggregation) | Any-candidate collision gaps | Gold-phone coverage | Exact gold-hypothesis collision positives |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| TRAIN-fit top 8 | 81,112 | 0.036681 | 6,795 / 10,139 (67.0%) | 23 / 46 (50.0%) | 2 / 46 |
| All 42 nonblank tokenizer symbols | 425,838 | 0.034919 | 10,139 / 10,139 (100%) | 46 / 46 (100%) | 2 / 46 |

Broadening candidates increases gold-phone coverage by 23 events but lowers AP-I by 0.001762 in this sparse population. It also makes every gap collision-participating under the broad “any candidate collides” definition, while exact gold-hypothesis collision support stays at two. This confirms that broad collision flags depend strongly on candidate inventory and cannot be interpreted as the share of positive gold hypotheses that are non-identifiable. The full tokenizer includes `ERR` and `SPN`; “42” means all nonblank token symbols, not 42 ordinary phonemes.

The full matched precision-recall coordinates are saved at `experiments/rq1_method_bench_05/agent_a/matched_exact_gap_pr_curves.csv`; method/subset supports, prevalence and AP are in `matched_exact_gap_pr_summary.json`. Curves include canonical E4 mean, E4 max, E3 and T2. On this 10,139-gap population, top-eight collision-participating versus identifiable subsets have prevalences 0.456% versus 0.449%. Canonical E4 mean AP-I is 0.02148 (31/6,795) versus 0.09181 (15/3,344); E4 max is 0.01692 and 0.09924; E3 is 0.00731 and 0.02171; T2 is 0.00367 and 0.00818. These estimates have low positive support, and similar prevalence means broad collision membership itself does not explain the E4 ranking gap.
