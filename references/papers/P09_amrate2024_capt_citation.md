## Abstract / Summary
This systematic review analyzes 30 peer-reviewed empirical journal articles published between 1999 and 2022 that investigate the effects of computer-assisted pronunciation training (CAPT) on healthy second-language (L2) learners' pronunciation. Using a pedagogically informed codebook mapped to L2 pronunciation teaching guidelines, the review evaluates instructional designs, target features, pedagogical tasks, and assessment paradigms. Key findings highlight that:
1. Most existing CAPT research disproportionately targets adult English language learners.
2. A heavy emphasis is placed on segmental features (phoneme substitutions, vowels, consonants) relative to suprasegmentals (stress, rhythm, intonation).
3. Pedagogical tasks predominantly employ mechanical listen-and-repeat or read-aloud drills.
4. Evaluation paradigms frequently rely on discrete phonetic accuracy rather than holistic communicative measures such as comprehensibility and intelligibility.
The review provides essential design recommendations for actionable educational feedback in modern CAPT systems.
